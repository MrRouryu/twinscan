Why this matters now

YouTube looks at the whole channel, not just one video.

“Content that looks like it's made with a template with little to no variation across videos.” YouTube Help, YPP monetization policies: “inauthentic content”

Since July 15, 2025, YouTube has used the term inauthentic content for mass-produced or repetitive uploads, and that content isn't eligible for monetization. Using AI is fine. Looking like a template isn't.

The hard part is that you can't see your own template. Every video feels different when you're the one making it. Twinscan sees the channel the way someone scrolling it for the first time would.

How it works

Paste a channel. See the pattern. Change one thing.

01

Scan

Paste your channel link. Twinscan pulls your last 10 to 50 public uploads, with no login and no access to your account.

02

Compare

Every upload is compared with every other one: thumbnail layout and colour, title structure, description boilerplate, runtime and posting rhythm. You get a sameness score for each and a side-by-side view of the closest pairs.

03

Fix before you publish

Before your next upload, drop in its title and thumbnail. Twinscan tells you if it's a near-twin of something you've already posted, and which part to change.

What Twinscan is

  • A mirror. It measures how similar your uploads are to each other.
  • A checklist of what to vary, based on YouTube's public policy text.
  • A weekly watch that flags when your channel starts drifting toward one template.

What it isn't

  • Not a prediction of YouTube's decision. Only YouTube makes that call.
  • Not a way to disguise copied or reused content.
  • Not affiliated with or endorsed by YouTube or Google.
